Here's the cruelest irony of homeschooling: the teacher and the nurse are the same person. And right now, she's the one who needs the nurse.

You're on the couch with a fever, a headache, and the special despair of knowing that the entire school day is sitting in *your* head — the plan, the materials, the energy to make it all happen — and your head is currently full of cotton.

So let's get one thing straight: today, school runs itself, or it doesn't run at all. Both are fine. Here's how.

## Step one: declare it

Say it out loud, to the kids and to yourself: "Mom is sick today. It's a couch day."

This matters more than you think. Kids take their cues from you — if you're trying to power through and visibly suffering, they'll be anxious *and* badly taught. If you declare it, make the couch nest, and hand over the plan, they'll settle. You're not ruining the day by resting. You're teaching them that sick people rest, which is honestly a more important lesson than long division.

## The survival plan (from someone who's been there)

**1. Lower the bar to the floor.** Not "light school day" low. Floor low. Today counts if: everybody ate, nobody got hurt, and something vaguely educational happened. That's the whole list.

**2. Make school self-running.** This is the entire game. You need activities that require zero teaching, zero explaining, zero hovering — because you are horizontal and will remain horizontal. Independent reading. Audiobooks. Educational shows (yes, really — a nature documentary is school today). Drawing prompts. And printed packets with answer keys, so there's no "moooom, is this right?"

**3. Feed them like it's a snow day.** Cereal for lunch. Snack plates for dinner. Paper plates so there's no dishes. Future you — healthy you — does not care what present sick you served. Let it go.

**4. Stagger the kids if you can.** If you have an older one, deputize them — not to teach, just to be the "captain" who reads the plan and keeps things moving. Kids rise to this beautifully. Pay them in extra screen time.

**5. Rest without performing rest.** Don't spend your tiny energy budget feeling guilty about resting. The guilt is the most exhausting part of a sick day, and it's completely optional. Cancel it.

## For the multi-kid chaos

If you've got more than one, the sick-mom day has a special challenge: they feed off each other. Two healthy kids + one sick mom = a situation that can go feral by noon.

The fix is structure, not supervision. Write the day's plan on one piece of paper and stick it to the fridge:

- 9:00 — Packets at the table
- 10:30 — Reading nests (separate rooms if possible — *separate rooms*)
- 11:30 — Show of mom's choice (nature documentary, not cartoons — pick your battles, but start strong)
- Lunch — snack plates, paper plates
- Afternoon — free choice, quiet voices, mom is sleeping

Separate rooms for the reading block is the pro move. Together they're a band; apart they're angels. You know this.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I homeschool when I'm sick?**
Declare a couch day, lower the bar to the floor, and use only self-running activities: independent reading, audiobooks, educational shows, and printed packets with answer keys. Nothing that requires teaching. Rest is the priority — the schoolwork will wait.

**What counts as school on a sick-mom day?**
Anything vaguely educational counts: reading, a documentary, drawing, a learning packet, journaling. If your state requires attendance records, log it as an independent study day. One day of light work has zero effect on long-term learning.

**How do I keep multiple kids occupied while I'm sick?**
Structure beats supervision. Post a simple schedule on the fridge, separate the kids for quiet blocks (separate rooms is the pro move), and deputize the oldest as captain. Self-running packets per kid mean nobody needs you to explain anything.

**Should I feel guilty about taking a sick day from homeschooling?**
No — and the guilt is the most exhausting part, so it's actively making you sicker. One rest day protects the whole year. The mom who rests today is the mom who still loves homeschooling in May.

**What if I'm sick for multiple days?**
Then it's a rest week, and that's okay too. Lean harder on the self-running stuff: a fresh generated packet per kid per day (that's what the unlimited plan is for — $9/month), library audiobooks, documentaries. Call in reinforcements if you have them. The curriculum will survive.
